# The thinking behind Product Excellence

Research reviewed on November 18, 2026. The skill is a contemporary adaptation of useful product habits, informed by Steve Jobs' recorded remarks, correspondence, and product examples. It is not a recovered Apple process or a simulation of Jobs.

## What the research changed

The earlier skill emphasized useful outcomes, careful implementation, and verification beyond the interface. Those remain essential. The revision adds a missing question: **is this a compelling way to solve the problem in the first place?**

The change is deliberately small in machinery. The installable instructions remain self-contained. There are no required workshops, scores, personas, research phases, or extra tools. The agent should spend its effort shaping the product and testing the consequential choices.

| Product habit | How the skill makes it actionable | What prevents misuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start with an experience worth creating | Describe a concrete moment of use before selecting features and technology. | Separate evidence of a need from an assumed solution or market. |
| Develop a point of view | Compare a straightforward approach with a meaningfully different mechanism when the brief is open, then choose. | Keep exploration proportional; do not reopen fixed designs or small repairs. |
| Connect ideas | Borrow a useful mechanism from another discipline, combine familiar parts, or question an inherited step. | Explain the transfer and its limits; novelty alone earns nothing. |
| Make focus serve quality | State the important choice, the tradeoff, and what is left out. | Cutting scope must not remove the core experience's value or useful expert control. |
| Give the experience character | Translate a desired quality into interaction, language, hierarchy, pacing, and feedback. | No universal visual style; beauty and play can be legitimate value. |
| Design the surrounding system | Check the relevant setup, content, integration, handoff, support, or return visit. | Do not expand a bounded task into an ecosystem project. |
| Make ideas tangible | Use a working slice, sample output, or walkthrough that can expose the key weakness. | A prototype must answer a question; requested implementation still needs completion. |
| Hold conviction and remain correctable | Recommend a direction and name evidence that could change it. | Inspect real results; do not invent feedback or hide failures. |

These instructions are our design choices. Their usefulness is evaluated in [the development study](EVALUATION.md); historical examples alone do not validate an agent skill.

## Source ledger

The sources below were opened and their available text inspected. Video playback was not part of this research. Original events and later publication dates are distinguished where relevant.

### Experience before technology; demonstration as proof

**Steve Jobs, WWDC Q&A, 1997.** [Recording and transcript, archival mirror](https://allaboutstevejobs.com/videos/keynotes/wwdc_1997_closing_chat).

In the customer-experience answer, Jobs connects product direction to customer benefit and describes showing a LaserWriter printout to make the value apparent. The later exchange also recognizes finite engineering capacity. This informed the experience-first opening and the instruction to demonstrate a real result. The mirror cautions that transcript timestamps may differ from the recording; it is not an Apple-hosted transcript.

### Focus, judgment, and changing one's mind

**Steve Jobs Archive, _Make Something Wonderful_.** [Official edited collection](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/).

The July 1998 Macworld address explains product-line focus in terms of concentrating people and shortening development cycles. The October 1995 Andy Grove correspondence records Jobs reversing a position after discussion. His May 1984 interview describes learning aesthetic judgment through experience and mistakes. Together these support focused execution and revisable judgment. The collection is selected and excerpted, not a controlled study of leadership effectiveness.

### Connections across disciplines

**Stanford commencement address, June 12, 2005.** [Official prepared text](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says).

Jobs relates his calligraphy study to Macintosh typography. The useful transfer is to look outside the immediate category for principles that improve a product. His retrospective account does not establish every counterfactual he makes about computing history, nor does it imply that an analogy is automatically useful.

### Care beyond bare functionality

**Steve Jobs Archive, _The Objects of Our Life_.** [1983 Aspen talk with context published July 18, 2024](https://stevejobsarchive.com/stories/objects-of-our-life).

The archival exhibit and Jony Ive's recollection emphasize accessible explanations, creative development, and care for the quality of designed objects. The revision therefore permits beauty, curiosity, and enjoyment to be part of the product's purpose. Ive's recollections, Jobs' statements, and the exhibit's editorial interpretation are different types of evidence.

### The supporting system is part of the product

**Apple, April 28, 2003.** [iTunes Music Store launch announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2003/04/28Apple-Launches-the-iTunes-Music-Store/).

The offering joined catalog access, previews, purchases, downloads, playback, devices, and usage rights. This provides a concrete example of value depending on technical and commercial coordination. It informed the whole-promise check. A launch announcement describes an offering; it is not independent proof of quality or a causal explanation for later success.

### Exploration can precede a product opportunity

**AllThingsD, June 1, 2010.** [Organizer's contemporaneous D8 liveblog](https://allthingsd.com/20100601/steve-jobs-session/).

The 6:59 p.m. entry reports Jobs describing touch-display and interaction experiments that helped suggest a phone. This complicates a rigid reading of “experience first”: technical exploration can reveal an opportunity. The benefit still needs a coherent product. The liveblog mixes quotations, paraphrases, and observations; it is not a verbatim transcript.

### Admired design is not proof of demand

**Apple, July 3, 2001.** [Power Mac G4 Cube production suspension](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2001/07/03Apple-Puts-Power-Mac-G4-Cube-on-Ice/).

Apple acknowledged that most customers chose its minitowers and suspended Cube production. The lesson is to test adoption separately from aesthetic conviction. The announcement also contradicts the literal claim that the Cube never sold.

## How the supplied article was used

Brad Barbera's _Steve Jobs: A Product Developer's Perspective_ (_PDMA Visions_, volume 36, number 1, 2012) was supplied by the repository owner and read in full. It helped frame customer needs versus suggested solutions, connections across disciplines, experimentation, and the total experience. The article itself is not redistributed here.

Its strongest contribution is challenging the idea that listening to customers means asking them to invent the product. Its historical claims require care: the Cube's supposed lack of any sales conflicts with Apple's announcement; claims about inventing entire industries are unnecessary to this skill; a quoted characterization of immutable leadership is counterbalanced by Jobs' recorded reversal. Time-bound observations from 2012 are not presented as current facts.

## What the skill does not inherit

- A famous person's taste as a substitute for evidence about this audience.
- A visual recipe of sparse screens, monochrome surfaces, or hidden controls.
- Harsh interpersonal behavior as a requirement for high standards.
- The assumption that technical novelty, a persuasive demo, or beautiful execution guarantees demand.
- A formula claiming to reproduce Apple's commercial results.

The useful inheritance is demanding care for the experience, the courage to choose, and the willingness to revise the work when reality disagrees.
