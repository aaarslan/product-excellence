## Recommendation

Do not commit to the six-month build yet. Run a small, time-boxed concierge test first. Quote-entry pain is credible; demand for this particular assistant, willingness to switch, and viable economics remain unproven.

### What the evidence supports

- Eight of twelve support conversations mentioned tedious entry. That describes this support sample, not 67% of the market: selection bias and the small sample prevent that extrapolation. Reporting pain is not choosing a product.
- Three spreadsheets substantiate 15–35 minutes for complex quotes. They do not establish how frequently those quotes occur, typical quote time across shops, or how much work automation can safely remove.
- Five owners withholding details creates uncertainty, not evidence for or against demand. Two offers to try something free are weak adoption signals. No payment commitment means willingness to pay is unknown, not necessarily absent.

The shop owner may be the buyer while staff do the entry. Observe both roles rather than assuming their incentives match.

### Compare the alternatives

Spreadsheets and email are familiar and already embedded in operations. Doing nothing avoids build and support costs but leaves demonstrated friction. A structured intake form, reusable quote template, or spreadsheet helper may remove much of that friction more cheaply. AI must earn its additional cost through better outcomes, not novelty.

Existing relationships offer a reachable pilot channel, but 30 shops are not proof of scalable distribution. Differentiation, repeat usage, data-sharing comfort, and support burden need evidence.

### Proposed next test, subject to your authorization

Over three weeks, offer up to five of those shops a manually assisted quoting workflow, without changing their existing delivery process. Cap internal work at 20 hours and incremental cash spending at $0. Do not contact anyone or run the test yet.

Use synthetic data for initial walkthroughs and only explicitly consented data for real workflows. Require shop review before any quote reaches a customer. Compare comparable quotes using each shop’s current process and the assisted process; record complexity and avoid claiming causal proof.

Measure total shop time, including review and corrections, material pricing/availability errors, operator time, repeat requests, and acceptance of a concrete paid continuation offer. Report counts with denominators, including invitations, participants, completed quotes, and payment commitments.

Set a gate before starting: at least three of five shops use it repeatedly, save at least 30% of total quote time, and commit to a price that covers $1 generation plus measured support and operating costs with a worthwhile margin. Any material quoting error requires repair and retesting. If the gate fails, stop or test the simpler alternative. Passing warrants a bounded prototype, not automatically six months of investment.
