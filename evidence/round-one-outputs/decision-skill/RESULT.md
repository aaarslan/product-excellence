# RelayDesk: next six-week commitment

**Decision:** Do not commit to the graphical rules builder. Commit to a bounded escalation-visibility improvement using existing thresholds: visible indicators in compact queues and a saved escalated-items view. Preserve the audit-log commitment. Use Northbank as the single design partner, not as a bespoke routing engagement.

**What we can promise:** A six-week visibility scope, with a permission check and Northbank walkthrough in week one, a proposed pilot by the end of week two if that gate passes, and the remaining period for measured use, fixes, and rollout readiness. Before promising a customer delivery date, validate permissions and the 2–4-day estimate. Keep existing threshold behavior and routing unchanged. Exclude nested rules, new classification data, working hours, and manual ticket monitoring. Do not promise renewal, expansion, or elimination of missed responses.

**Why this commitment:** In two observed shifts, 12 of 19 late tickets already had escalation flags that compact view concealed. This supports fixing visibility first, not a claim that the fix would prevent 12 breaches. Four other breaches involve staffing hours and three missing data; neither is solved by a rules editor alone. Eighteen of Northbank's 27 spreadsheet rules repeat the same threshold, while the frequency and importance of the nine exceptions remain unknown. Three other customers want visibility, and support reports explanatory overhead. These are promising reuse signals, not proven adoption or savings. The 29 customers who never enabled thresholds may have different obstacles.

Northbank's existing $48,000 ARR is material, about 7% of current company ARR, but operational fit is only one renewal condition. The $24,000 expansion and $18,000 prospect are uncommitted and do not justify capacity as contracted revenue. There are eight nominal engineer-weeks available after audit logs, with routing expertise concentrated in one engineer. A 7–10-week builder estimate already strains that capacity before hardening, conflicts, migration, support, and the separately estimated working-hours work. No automated routing rollback further increases delivery risk. Doing nothing leaves a demonstrated obstruction; visibility is the lower-cost, reusable intervention.

**Decision-changing test:** Use next Tuesday's offered 30-minute session with the operations manager and shift supervisor to replay urgent-ticket handling in compact and saved views, including a hidden flag, a mistaken classification, and an off-hours arrival. Ask them to identify which exceptions are renewal-critical and whether visibility meets their operational requirement. This memo authorizes no outreach; the account owner should separately establish the consolidation decision and renewal criteria with procurement.

Cap the first engineering stage at five engineer-days, including permission validation and a usable pilot. If it exceeds that cap, return to planning rather than quietly consume the six weeks. For a two-week pilot, propose these provisional decision thresholds: both supervisors can find and act on escalations without extra permissions; no access leakage or routing regression; the view is used on at least 80% of staffed shifts; and the manager accepts it as operationally sufficient with no recurring critical exception left unaddressed. Record escalated-ticket response times and breach causes against a comparable baseline; do not infer a causal improvement from the original two shifts. Support is limited to one weekly review.

**Next decision:** If these conditions hold, finish the visibility release and pursue renewal on that scope. If repeated, consequential exceptions defeat it, estimate one narrow missing capability and seek a concrete commercial commitment before changing the roadmap, rather than defaulting to a builder. Sparse usage, insufficient ticket volume, or unresolved procurement means ambiguous evidence: retain the small fix, make no broader promise, and use the remaining window only to resolve the specific gap. A failed permission or safety check blocks rollout until repaired.

*Basis: supplied planning packet only. No customer contact, runtime verification, or system changes performed.*
