# Decision: visibility first, no six-week rules-builder promise

## Immediate commitment
Commit the next six weeks to a narrowly scoped escalation-visibility release and one Northbank design-partner pilot, while preserving the enterprise audit-log commitment. Do not commit to a graphical rules builder, working-hours automation, or custom routing exceptions.

Promise a dated demonstration and a go/no-go decision on the visibility pilot next Tuesday, not an unconditional production delivery date this week. Subject to validating Northbank’s permissions and the estimate, target the compact-view indicator and saved escalation view in the first two weeks, then use the remaining window to measure and correct the pilot. The 2–4-day estimate covers the saved view; confirm the combined scope before promising it. Keep routing semantics unchanged and support limited to a weekly review, not manual ticket monitoring.

## Why this commitment
Northbank represents $48,000 ARR, about 7% of current recurring revenue. Retention deserves action, but neither the $24,000 expansion estimate nor the $18,000 prospect is committed revenue. Operational improvements cannot guarantee renewal while procurement’s consolidation review remains unresolved.

The strongest observed problem is that existing escalations are hidden: 12 of 19 breached tickets were already flagged. That supports testing visibility before buying configurability, not claiming a 63% reduction in breaches. Four after-hours cases and three missing-data cases will not be solved by this release. Eighteen of the spreadsheet’s 27 rules repeat thresholds across customer names; the value and frequency of the other exceptions remain unproven.

Visibility also addresses requests from three other customers and may reduce support effort, although neither benefit is quantified. Non-use by 29 customers is a question, not evidence of demand for a builder.

Only eight engineer-weeks remain after the audit-log commitment, and routing expertise is concentrated in one engineer. A builder already needs 7–10 engineer-weeks before hardening and excluded work; working hours adds another 2–3. Promising it now would jeopardize existing obligations and introduce routing risk without automated rollback.

## Decision-changing next step
Use Tuesday’s 30-minute session with the operations manager and shift supervisor to show the saved-view workflow, validate permissions and who watches it, and agree on an operational acceptance test. Review representative exceptions and ask whether visibility would materially satisfy the operational renewal objection. Separately, have the account owner establish procurement’s consolidation criteria and decision date; Tuesday’s attendees cannot settle that gate.

If the workflow fits, propose a two-week pilot after release. Compare staffed-hour breach rates and time from escalation to acknowledgement with a comparable baseline, keeping after-hours and missing-data cases separate. Agree the minimum acceptable improvement with Northbank before launch; two observed shifts do not justify a numerical success promise today. Check results weekly and decide by week six, ahead of renewal.

If Northbank demonstrates frequent, consequential exceptions that visibility cannot address, reconsider one bounded capability only after engineering sizes safe delivery and the commercial owner establishes a credible retention or paid-expansion case. If success requires the full builder, explicitly decline the six-week deadline rather than quietly expanding scope. What we can promise is the bounded evaluation and, once feasibility is checked, the visibility release. We cannot yet promise reduced breaches, renewed revenue, or general-purpose rules.
