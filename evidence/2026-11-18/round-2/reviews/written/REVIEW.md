# Independent review: Case 2 photo-scanning pilot

## Scope and basis

I read only the six authorized files: inputs/case-2/TASK.md, inputs/case-2/CONTEXT.md, inputs/judge/RUBRIC.md, and variants/case-2/A/RESULT.md, B/RESULT.md, and C/RESULT.md. References below use TASK, CONTEXT, RUBRIC, A, B, and C followed by the source line number(s). No identities, other cases, previous results, other reviewers, outside sources, or services were inspected.

I checked the complete recommendations, counted their entire texts using whitespace-delimited words, and independently recomputed their appointment and staff allocations. I did not perform a technical rehearsal, contact participants, book sessions, purchase materials, or verify demand. For this case the recommendation is the artifact; a pass means a credible, bounded plan, not an observed successful service (RUBRIC:19, 25–30, 78).

## Common arithmetic and feasibility checks

The provided resource is one volunteer from Wednesday 10:00 to 12:30 in five weeks, with week four unavailable, and 90 paid minutes per week including administration, preparation, and closing (CONTEXT:24–25). Every candidate uses week one for preparation and weeks two, three, five, and six for public appointments.

| Check | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public capacity | 4 Wednesdays × 2 = 8 appointments | 4 × 2 = 8 | 4 × 2 = 8 |
| Appointment minutes per public Wednesday | 2 × 55 = 110 | 2 × 60 = 120 | 2 × 60 = 120 |
| Remaining volunteer time | 10 setup + 10 turnover + 20 closing = 40 | 10 setup + 10 turnover + 10 closing = 30 | 10 setup + 10 turnover + 10 closing = 30 |
| Total volunteer window | 110 + 40 = 150 minutes | 120 + 30 = 150 | 120 + 30 = 150 |
| Allocated paid time | 30 + 20 + 20 + 20 = 90 minutes | 45 + 30 + 15 = 90 | 30 + 30 + 30 = 90 |
| Protected final handoff time per appointment | 15 minutes | 20 minutes | 15 minutes |

Sources: A:9, 19, 25; B:3, 22, 26; C:11, 15, 29. Each schedule fits; none has an arithmetic defect. The first-week paid allowance is explicitly retained rather than treated as an additional staffing grant (A:19; B:22; C:15).

At the supplied 60–90 seconds per image, ten images require roughly 10–15 minutes of scanning alone; five require 5–7.5 minutes. Those figures do **not** establish appointment capacity: choosing, handling, naming, transfer, coaching, and reset are additional work, and the observed trials were volunteer-operated (CONTEXT:15–17). All three plans accommodate that distinction through narrow scope, substantially longer appointments, preflight, and protected handoff time. Their actual full-cycle performance remains unverified.

The eight places are capacity, not eight known customers. The enquiry log was not deduplicated; availability was not linked to collection type; device information is missing; the history group's interest may overlap (CONTEXT:7–10). None of the candidates adds these counts into a demand forecast.

Materials expenditure is a proposed prelaunch check in all three, not an established price or purchase. The proposals can comply with £300 because launch and quantities are conditional on pricing within it. There is no evidence that any particular drive stock, connector assortment, or printed material has already been secured.

## Pairwise comparison

### A versus B — A preferred, moderate confidence

1. **Earlier test of the observed failure point.** A tests export on the receiving laptop after the first priority image (A:25); B places export/checking in the final 20 minutes (B:26). Both have credible recovery controls, but A can detect a problem before most of the participant's appointment has been spent.
2. **Stronger evidence of usable completion inside the appointment.** A has the participant open the files and find the folder on their own laptop (A:25). B reopens exports and permits an explanation of subsequent access (B:28), relying more heavily on prebooking destination confirmation.

This helps a participant establish that their particular copies work on their actual receiving device during the supported session. The cost is A's computer/helper requirement. B can serve someone with a verified usable destination that cannot conveniently be brought to the library (B:9–11). That access advantage makes the preference modest; the notes do not quantify how many residents each eligibility rule would exclude.

### A versus C — Tie, moderate confidence

The strongest outcome mechanism is shared: a first-priority photograph is exported and opened immediately on the participant's receiving computer, followed by protected handoff time and a checked take-home result (A:25–27; C:27–31).

A allows up to ten, prioritizes five, and treats independence as optional; it can produce a somewhat larger useful collection without guaranteeing ten (A:3, 25). C makes five the full offer, explicitly gates launch on the complete timed path, and reviews the first pair before releasing later places (C:3, 11, 21). A's flexibility and C's tighter initial commitment are both defensible. The supplied evidence does not establish that either scope creates superior net value.

The 55-versus-60-minute appointments and different closing buffers do not break the tie: both schedules fit, and their actual handling/reset needs are unknown. Both impose essentially the same receiving-computer access burden.

### B versus C — C preferred, moderate confidence

1. **C verifies the destination immediately.** Its first photograph opens on the participant's own computer (C:27), while B's destination is qualified beforehand and export checking occurs later (B:20, 26).
2. **C has a stronger in-session completion check.** Opening all five on the receiving computer is part of the defined finish and final check (C:3, 29); B permits a verbal explanation of future access (B:28). C also contains the unproven process with a complete timed preflight and first-pair review before later places are released (C:11, 21).

C gives this small, unknown-demand pilot a clear test of whether a resident actually leaves able to access the selected photographs. Its cost is a smaller collection and a more restrictive attendance requirement. B's broader destination eligibility and larger ceiling could matter substantially to residents; neither actual demand mix nor observed service success is available to establish the size of that tradeoff.

## Material observations still unverified

- **Eligible demand and attendance:** No firm appointment exists in the source. Wednesday availability, collection type, receiving-device access, and willingness to bring a computer/helper are not linked in the enquiry evidence (CONTEXT:7–10).
- **Volunteer reliability and future capacity:** The existing offer supports planning; it does not verify attendance on every date or any commitment beyond six weeks (CONTEXT:24, 27).
- **End-to-end technical success:** USB export worked once; other devices/routes and all proposed rehearsal and recovery procedures remain untested here (CONTEXT:15–17, 22). A and C's receiving-computer checks are proposed actions, not observations.
- **Full-cycle workload:** The arithmetic is correct, but bookings, compatibility checks, cancellations, instruction, file checking, cleanup, follow-up, and exceptions have not been timed under these offers. All three must count that work against their actual weekly allocations.
- **Materials:** Quantities, compatibility, prices, and availability need confirmation before spending. The cost cap is credible as a condition, not a verified shopping basket.
- **Independent ability and later use:** A supervised scan is a useful observation, not proof of independent home scanning. A later-use question can gather evidence, but unanswered follow-ups cannot establish success.
- **Access effects:** A and C may exclude residents B could serve. B's conditional route flexibility may increase verification work. The source does not establish either the number affected or the workload difference.

These limits do not prevent judging the recommendations themselves. Each offers a workable, evidence-aware pilot with a credible handoff condition and honest boundaries. A and C have a modest advantage in proving the participant's actual access early; their differing scope choices are equally defensible on the available facts.
