# Notes for the planning meeting

These are fictional service-planning materials. The pilot's purpose is to help people save photographs they care about in a form they can actually take away and use. The manager also hopes participants might feel able to continue afterwards. There is no target for scanning the largest possible number of images.

## What residents have said

- Staff logged 24 enquiries in six weeks, probably from about 19 people; the log was not deduplicated. No one has been offered or accepted a firm appointment.
- In ten informal follow-up conversations, four people wanted roughly 10–20 special prints copied. Three had boxes of about 150–400 prints. Two actually needed help getting existing photographs off a phone. One wanted to scan slides.
- Four of those ten said weekday daytime would work, three wanted evenings or Saturdays, and three did not discuss times. We did not record which collection sizes went with which availability.
- A local history group offered to tell its members about a trial. Its coordinator says eight people are interested. Some may be among the enquiries already logged. We do not know how many would attend or what devices they use.
- One resident said, “I'd be happy if I could send Mum just a few of these.” Another asked whether they could leave a whole box and collect it later. A third mainly wanted someone beside them the first time.

## What happened in two informal trials

- One participant left with 18 scanned prints on their own USB drive after 55 minutes.
- A second had nine prints scanned in 45 minutes, but transfer to their phone failed. They left without digital copies; the trial files were subsequently deleted with their agreement.
- The same volunteer did nearly all the operating in both trials. We have not observed someone working independently after an introduction. Scanning itself took roughly 60–90 seconds per image; choosing, handling, naming, and transferring took variable extra time.

## Resources and constraints

- One working flatbed scanner for prints, one library laptop, and a table in a small room. The scanner does not handle slides. We have no tested phone-backup service.
- The laptop has a guest account that clears files on logout. USB export worked in the trial. Browser uploads and connections to other phone models have not been tested. People may bring their own laptops, but installing and using this scanner on them has not been tested either.
- The library does not want to retain participants' photographs or account passwords after a session. Anything offered must have a dependable way for participants to leave with their copies.
- One volunteer can offer **Wednesday 10:00–12:30**, for five of the six weeks. They are away in week four. The room is available at those times. Another person has expressed interest in volunteering but has not committed.
- Paid staff can contribute **90 minutes total per week**, including administration, preparation, and closing down. They can handle ordinary room bookings, but cannot provide unplanned technical support at the desk. No evening or Saturday staffing has been allocated to this pilot.
- Up to **£300** is available for materials. Equipment above is already owned. There is no budget for additional paid staffing or custom software. The pilot will be free to participants.
- The manager can adjust the scope and booking arrangements. They cannot promise the room, volunteer, or budget beyond six weeks yet.
