# Recommendation: “Ready on Saturday”

Run a four-week trial with the **carpet cleaner and projector only**. Lend on Saturdays 1 and 3; receive and check returns on Saturdays 2 and 4. Promise a complete, checked item at an agreed time. Reserve only equipment already inside the cupboard and checked—not something another borrower has promised to return.

This deliberately limits the trial to four possible loans. The intervening week protects the promise, but reduces availability; whether residents tolerate that tradeoff is part of the trial.

## What a resident experiences

A pinned WhatsApp notice lists the two items, actual collection and return dates, and the request deadline. Residents privately message the duty volunteer in WhatsApp by Wednesday 18:00: “Carpet cleaner, this Saturday.” No catalogue, account or new app is needed. Allocate available items in request order, one item per household per round.

By Thursday 18:00, a successful requester receives: “Yes—the cleaner is checked and reserved. Collect Saturday at 10:10; return next Saturday at 10:00. Reply here if you need to cancel.” A public round-closed notice states that requests without private confirmation are unavailable; nobody should travel without confirmation. It contains no borrower information.

At collection, volunteer and resident check the contents card together. The cleaner has short emptying, washing and packing instructions; the projector’s card includes its cable. At return, the volunteer checks completeness, condition and basic operation before marking it ready again.

A cancellation can pass to the next requester within the administration allowance. A late or incomplete return blocks that item’s next reservation. Send one private recovery message offering the next remaining trial session; do not make a replacement promise. An overdue after the final session remains an unresolved recovery obligation; no extra access is assumed.

## What we operate

Use one restricted spreadsheet, accessible only to the two volunteers, recording borrower, item, agreed times, condition and return status. One duty volunteer controls reservations; the other uses the same record when covering. Keep requests in existing WhatsApp private messages. Publish no names, contacts or borrowing histories. Remove identifiable trial records after returns and outstanding issues are resolved; retain aggregate results.

Preparation, capped at six hours: two hours checking equipment; one writing and printing contents/instruction cards; one setting up the record and message templates; one rehearsing collection, missing cable and overdue scenarios; one confirming dates and announcing the trial. Exclude equipment that cannot be made ready within that allowance. Budget £20 for printing/cards; leave £100 uncommitted.

Weekly time ceiling: 45 minutes at the cupboard, 20 handling requests and confirmations, 10 for reminders/recovery, 10 for records and review, five contingency. Close intake when its allowance is exhausted. All handovers stay within Saturday 10:00–10:45. If a Saturday is unavailable, announce the permitted Thursday replacement in advance, using **17:30–18:00**, within centre opening hours; confirm affected residents can attend.

## Why this, and the decision afterwards

The request trail in *EVIDENCE.md* records 11 of 23 requests for these two items, conflicting availability answers, failed handovers and late returns disrupting later requests. These support testing reliable fulfilment; three expressions of app interest do not establish app demand.

Record requests, declines and reasons, completed intended uses, broken confirmations, late/incomplete returns and actual volunteer minutes. Saturday workers remain underserved; log that limitation without assuming Thursday suits them.

Continue if residents actually benefit, confirmed loans work and effort fits the ceiling. Change the cadence if the holding week or access hours repeatedly prevent useful borrowing. Stop new lending if readiness, dependable handovers or the time ceiling cannot be maintained. Four possible loans can expose operational failures, not establish community-wide demand.
