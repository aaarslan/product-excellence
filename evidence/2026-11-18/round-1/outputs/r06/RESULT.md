## Recommendation

Run a four-week **“ready-to-borrow Saturday” service** for the carpet cleaner and projector: one-week loans, private WhatsApp requests, and confirmed appointments. Pause new reservations for the other items during the trial so their administration cannot overwhelm the same volunteer allowance.

These two items generated 11 of 23 visible requests. Conflicting availability, missed handovers, unanswered requests and the missing projector cable point toward a service coordination problem. The three expressions of interest in an app do not establish demand for one. The request trail is incomplete, so this is a small operational experiment.

## A resident’s experience

A weekly group notice lists the two items, the collection window and the request deadline, without borrower information. Residents privately message the posting volunteer’s existing WhatsApp account with the item and intended use date. This is the sole request route; there is no new account or inbox.

Requests close Tuesday at 18:00; the volunteer gives a definite answer by Wednesday at 18:00. Allocate each item to its first eligible request. Eligibility includes being able to collect and return during the scheduled sessions. Record unsuccessful requests and their reasons privately.

**Confirm only equipment already checked and physically in the cupboard.** An expected return never becomes a firm booking. The confirmation states the collection appointment, return appointment, accessories and cleaning requirements. A resident needing the cleaner before Sunday can accept the Saturday arrangement or receive a clear refusal before making plans.

At collection, volunteer and resident check the kit together. The resident takes a printed pack-up card and receives one private return reminder. At return, the volunteer checks condition and completeness before marking it available again. Incomplete or questionable equipment stays unavailable.

## What we operate

Use Saturday 10:00–10:45 for booked collections, returns and checks. Thursday is a replacement only if Saturday is unavailable; its usable window is **17:30–18:00**, because the centre closes at 18:00 despite the volunteer’s longer offer. Publish the four-week calendar before taking requests. A replacement requires affected residents’ agreement; otherwise cancel their booking explicitly.

Budget each week for 45 minutes of handovers, 20 minutes of request replies, 10 minutes of reminders, 10 minutes of records and five minutes of contingency. One volunteer owns requests; the covering volunteer uses the shared register. Log actual minutes, including cancellations and chasing. Close intake before exceeding the weekly allowance.

The volunteers-only spreadsheet records item status, borrower contact, appointments, accessories, condition and completion. The group sees service information only. Remove pilot contact records after returns and unresolved issues are settled; retain anonymous totals.

Preparation totals six hours: reconcile existing loans and commitments (one hour), inspect both kits (two), prepare pack-up/check cards (one), establish and rehearse the register (one), and publish the calendar and instructions (one). Set a £40 ceiling for printing and sleeves; leave £80 unspent. Launch only if equipment and existing return commitments fit the plan.

## Limits and decision

This conservative arrangement permits at most four one-week loans: returned equipment waits for a subsequent booking cycle. Week four accepts returns only, avoiding obligations beyond the trial. Saturday workers remain excluded unless an available replacement suits them; record that as an access problem.

Continue this small service if at least three households complete loans, every confirmed collection succeeds, all items are returned complete by the final session, and every week stays within 90 minutes. Change scope or seek a different access agreement if unmet requests cluster around timing or unavailable items. Stop this format if equipment cannot be checked safely, workload exceeds the cap, or fewer than two eligible requests arrive. Four loans cannot establish long-term reliability or rule out wider demand.
