# Make Story Break worth opening

Please improve the supplied `index.html` into a small, complete offline browser experience my family would actually want to use. Implement it; don't just review the prototype.

We use one older laptop at the kitchen table. My children are 9 and 13, and adults join in too. We want to get from “I don't know what to write” to starting a short story quickly, then finish something we can read aloud in about ten minutes. The rough prototype already deals three story prompts, has a writing area, and keeps stories on this laptop. It works, but the experience feels more like filling in a form than making a story.

Use the notes in `EVIDENCE.md` to make your own decisions about the experience. There is room to change its structure and invent a better way to use the prompts. Aim for one coherent little product, with enough character to feel inviting without talking down to the older child or adult.

Requirements:

- Deliver a working, self-contained `index.html` using plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It must open directly from disk and work offline, with no dependencies or fetched assets.
- Keep the central activity: use a character, place, and complication to make a short story; keep a draft and finished stories on this device; reopen a finished story to read it.
- Existing drafts and saved stories must remain readable. The storage keys and an example old snapshot are supplied. You may improve the data model while retaining compatibility.
- Make it practical with a keyboard and at a narrow window width as well as on a laptop. No accounts, server, microphone access, or tracking.
- You may change or remove the countdown. We don't need a large writing suite, AI text generation, points economy, or social feed.

Spend your effort on the usable artifact. In your final response, identify the file, explain the main experience decisions briefly, and say what you actually checked. Work within a roughly fifteen-minute implementation budget.
