Recommendation

Use the five-day frontend/design budget to make decisions informed and outcomes truthful, not a redesign.

1. Fix false success first. Consume authoritative status, refresh deadline-sensitive screens, and reconcile action responses before showing success. Disable unavailable actions and distinguish a recorded decline from a replacement already accepted or packed. Preserve the alternative-selection path and clearly label it as awaiting picker confirmation, not a guaranteed replacement.
2. Put original and replacement unit prices, package sizes, and resulting line total beside Accept/Decline. Shoppers should not need a separate view to understand the cost and quantity tradeoff.
3. Replace “We'll ask before making a change” with explicit checkout and proposal disclosures: after three minutes without a response, the suggestion is automatically accepted unless substitutions are prohibited. Make the existing No substitutions choice clear. Disclosure corrects the promise; it does not establish informed consent or solve missed notifications.

Defer scheduling changes, longer waits, and default timeout changes: they require unavailable backend/operations capacity and carry unmeasured fulfillment tradeoffs. Commission that policy decision separately, including whether shoppers should explicitly choose timeout behavior.

Assess impact

Exercise on-time approval, pending alternatives, and a late decline across the deadline. Verify displayed outcomes against event logs. Repeat timed usability tasks for cost comprehension and timeout expectations. Compare pre/post misleading-success events and cost/ineffective-decline contacts per proposal, alongside refund and pickup-delay guardrails. Treat trends as directional, not causal proof. Push visibility, operational delay, total dissatisfaction, and which intervention helps most remain unknown; 500 timeouts are not evidence of consent.
