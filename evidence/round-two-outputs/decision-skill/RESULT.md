# Decision: improve export reliability before committing to automatic posting

Commit one engineering week to the export package: stable batch identifiers, mapping validation, and visible approval status, preserving optional approvals and existing workflows. Use the remaining window for two support-assisted pilot sessions and bounded integration feasibility work, not a promised production connector. Keep the second engineer on the required supplier-format change.

The strongest evidence is the customer's actual work: eight of 44 files needed edits, including mapping corrections and removal of unapproved adjustments. Automating today's export path could post those errors directly. The observed 18-minute clean batch establishes handling friction, not the claimed two-day close delay. Repeated downloads across customers suggest an investigation opportunity, not proof of failed exports.

The strongest counterargument is commercial: a $3,200-monthly customer offers $9,000, and seven customers share its accounting platform. But the offer is unsigned, recurring-price authority is absent, and prospects have not established demand for automatic posting. A three-week estimate excludes reconciliation and duplicate prevention, so it cannot support a four-week delivery promise.

Do not promise automatic posting, faster close, universal mandatory approvals, or accounting reconciliation support. Draft journals are a possible safer boundary, not a complete solution: approval and tool switching remain.

Next, inspect representative corrected exports against API transaction coverage and observe the pilot's mapping, approval, and import steps. Reconsider a narrowly scoped paid draft-journal pilot if compatibility, recovery ownership, delivery effort, and buyer commitment support explicit obligations without creating ongoing reconciliation services. Otherwise retain the cross-customer export improvement.
