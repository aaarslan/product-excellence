# Recommendation

Commit the four-week window to a safer, measurable export pilot, not automatic accounting posting. Spend one week adding stable batch identifiers, mapping validation, and explicit approval status using existing data. Pilot with the requesting customer through two supported sessions; use the remaining capacity for fixes, measurement, and bounded integration discovery. Keep approval-policy changes customer-specific rather than imposing mandatory approvals across the base.

The strongest evidence is workflow behavior: eight of 44 files needed edits, including three containing adjustments awaiting approval. Automating those exports could accelerate incorrect or premature postings. The three-week integration estimate excludes reconciliation and duplicate prevention, and transaction compatibility is unknown. The claimed two-day close delay has not been attributed to importing; the observed 18-minute batch was unusually clean. Repeated downloads likewise do not establish an automation need.

The strongest counterargument is commercial: this customer contributes 10% of current monthly revenue, offers $9,000, and has a plausible recurring pain. However, the offer is unsigned, does not establish recurring willingness to pay, and the two prospects have not validated automatic posting. Only seven customers share this accounting platform.

Do not promise automatic posting, a two-day close improvement, or a production integration date. A paid discovery pilot is reasonable only with explicit deliverables, acceptance criteria, and exclusions for bookkeeping and production posting.

Next, trace a full close and compare export transaction types with API support. Reconsider integration if measured handling savings, draft-journal suitability, recurring buyer commitment, and a bounded reconciliation/idempotency estimate justify displacing further export work.
