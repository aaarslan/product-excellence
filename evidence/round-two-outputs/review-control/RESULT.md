# Pickup substitution review

Use the five-day budget for informed decisions and truthful outcomes, not longer picker waits.

1. Prioritize authoritative status handling: refresh status when opening a proposal and before reporting an action’s outcome. Disable expired controls and distinguish a successful decline from an already accepted or packed item. Never label an ineffective decline “Order updated.”
2. Show original and replacement unit prices, package sizes, and resulting line totals beside Accept/Decline, using existing data. Explain that alternatives need picker confirmation.
3. Replace “We'll ask before making a change” with plain disclosure at checkout and on proposals: suggestions automatically proceed after three minutes without a response, except items marked “No substitutions.” Explain that existing option before checkout. This corrects the promise; it does not establish consent or resolve whether automatic acceptance is appropriate.

Defer timeout-policy and scheduling changes to a backend/operations follow-up. Automatic decline could increase missing items; longer waits could threaten slots, but neither tradeoff is quantified. Also defer visual polish unrelated to these decisions.

Assess with time-pressured usability sessions, including expired proposals and alternative requests, then a monitored rollout. Compare unexpected-cost and ineffective-decline contacts per proposal, authoritative late-action outcomes, and shopper comprehension. Track refunds, fulfillment, and pickup punctuality as guardrails; do not treat fewer contacts alone as success.

The 500 timeouts among 1,200 proposals do not prove shoppers saw notifications. Push receipt is unmeasured, contacts may overlap, and five sessions cannot rank intervention impact. Confirm endpoint semantics and available outcome reporting before committing the sprint scope.
